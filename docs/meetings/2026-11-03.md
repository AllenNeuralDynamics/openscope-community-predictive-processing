## Meeting Notes
**Manuscript Figure Structure:** Jerome presented a reorganized draft of the data-release manuscript, consolidating contributions into 10 main figures and requesting feedback on logical flow, figure placement, and supplementary material. 

Figure Consolidation: Jerome reviewed how approximately 15–20 candidate figures were reorganized into 10 main figures to fit the expected manuscript limit. The revised figures combine related content, use a more consistent visual style, and are intended to form the core of the data-release manuscript. 

Document Workflow: The manuscript platform can export directly to PDF through its export function. Jerome offered to make the download control point to the PDF so that Lucas and others can review and edit a fixed version, while interactive views remain available but static figures are now the default. 

Methods And Dataset Figures: The opening figures cover the experimental design, collected experiments, multimodal recording workflow, raw data, unit extraction, and representative traces. Jerome plans to move the detailed session tables to supplementary material because they are useful references but occupy substantial space. 

Writing Work: Jerome said the abstract and introduction have received substantial editing, but the results section still needs attention. He will contact contributors to help review and finalize the text now that the figure sequence is clearer. 

**Cross-Modality Quality Metrics:** Jerome, Ido, David, Lucas, and Farzaneh discussed how signal quality should be represented across mesoscope, SLAP2, and Neuropixels figures, with emphasis on avoiding arbitrary categorical thresholds and harmonizing metric definitions. 

SNR Presentation: Jerome proposed modality-specific figures showing signal-to-noise and related quality metrics for mesoscope, SLAP2, and Neuropixels. He questioned whether low, intermediate, and high SNR categories could lead data users to treat arbitrary boundaries as fixed scientific thresholds. 

Continuous Distributions: Ido presented an alternative SLAP2 version using a continuous gradient based on median event amplitude rather than discrete classes. The plot distinguishes glutamate-only experiments from experiments containing glutamate and calcium, and Ido agreed to prepare variants, potentially including a cumulative plot to harmonize the modality figures. 

Interpretation Of Trends: Jerome favored a representation that shows the continuous distribution and possible improvement in signal quality across more recent experiments, while avoiding an interpretation that two-channel recordings necessarily cause a signal-quality penalty. He planned to consult Kaspar before settling the explanatory text. 

Figure Review: Lucas noted that event-amplitude bars previously shown in an example trace were missing and would help readers understand the metric calculation. Jerome agreed to consider adding them and to harmonize colors and visual explanations across the quality-control panels. 

Neuropixels Labels: David asked about the additional Kilosort unit label because including it could substantially reduce the number of units retained for analysis. Jerome explained that it identifies more mixed units and proposed explaining it in the text or adding a panel; Sarah’s group will review and iterate the figure. 

**Receptive Field Analysis:** Lucas, Marcel, Nicholas, and Jerome reviewed the receptive-field figure, its session selection and stimulus comparisons, and possible integration with Nicholas’s longitudinal receptive-field and cell-registration analyses. 

Figure Content: Lucas explained that the figure compares receptive fields elicited by Gabor and Zebra stimuli across Neuropixels, mesoscope, and SLAP2 data. It includes representative examples, proportions of units with significant fields under each method, field centers, field areas, and response-strength measures. 

Analysis Scope: The current examples are drawn from different sessions than the SNR figures, and the team plans to harmonize the session choices. Lucas noted that the example receptive fields were selected using a significance-related ranking, so the team may need to decide how to describe their representative nature. 

Supplementary Material: Marcel noted that the current figure focuses on visual cortical areas, while receptive fields are also found in LGN and other regions. Jerome agreed that those additional results could be placed in supplementary material if supported by accompanying text. 

Related Analyses: Nicholas described an analysis tracking receptive-field properties in primary and lateral visual cortex across recording sessions, along with a pipeline for registering mesoscope sessions to identify individual neurons across days. Nicholas will post results to the GitHub discussion and present them the following week so the group can determine overlap with the current figure and whether the work belongs in the manuscript or a follow-up study. 

**Cell-Type And Oddball Figures:** The team reviewed reorganized cell-type and standard-oddball figures, including waveform and optotagging classifications and a cross-modality analysis of responses to mismatch stimuli. 

Cell-Type Figure: Jerome reorganized Sarah’s waveform-map material to emphasize waveform clusters, visual-cortex results, and overlap with optotagging. Larger panels were divided into supplementary figures covering waveforms across areas, extracted statistics, and optotagging definitions; Sarah indicated agreement with the reorganization. 

Oddball Figure Design: David explained that the standard-oddball figure now covers Neuropixels, mesoscope, and SLAP2 rather than only Neuropixels. Columns represent modalities, while rows show example units or ROIs, population heat maps, and quantitative responsiveness measures. 

Two Responsiveness Tests: David described Q1 as comparing the deviant with the preceding expected standard and Q2 as comparing the same event in the mismatch context with a matched control block. This distinction separates genuine mismatch-related responses from ordinary stimulus responses that occur in both conditions. 

Cross-Modality Patterns: Raster-map clustering showed similar groups across the three modalities, including units suppressed by mismatch and units with increased activity. The interactive version allows users to select mismatch types such as omissions, inspect surrounding trials, and examine responsive populations across areas. 

Data-Release Scope: David said the figure is intended to demonstrate what signals are available in the released data rather than establish a complete biological interpretation. He also discussed a possible table identifying which neurons respond to which stimuli, allowing users to apply their own responsiveness definitions. 

**Scientific Claim Review:** Farzaneh urged the group to distinguish data visualization from biological claims, and Jerome agreed to identify specific panels for focused review and use cautious language about predictive-processing interpretations. 

Panel-Level Review: Farzaneh requested a second review of panels that make biological or scientific claims, distinguishing them from panels that primarily document pipelines or visualize data. She asked Jerome to provide a concrete list of figures and panels so reviewers can focus on the relevant claims. 

Predictive-Processing Language: Farzaneh cautioned that example responses alone are insufficient to establish prediction signals or predictive coding effects because stronger analyses must address whether effects emerge reliably and depend on environmental statistics. Jerome agreed that the manuscript should describe results as aligned with such interpretations without claiming that the data establish them. 

Discussion Paragraph: Jerome invited Farzaneh to contribute a paragraph to the discussion explaining the limitations of the evidence and the distinction between observed signals and conclusions about predictive processing. The group will also review the corresponding results text for appropriately cautious wording. 

**Behavior And Supplementary Figures:** Jerome proposed moving the behavior figure earlier in the manuscript, retaining the oddball figure as the conclusion, and refining supplementary figures for hardware, trajectories, optotagging definitions, and interactive stimulus exploration. 

Behavior Figure Placement: Jerome suggested moving the running and behavior figure from the end to an earlier position, potentially as Figure 3, so the manuscript introduces animal behavior before neuronal response analyses and concludes with the standard-oddball results. 

Supplementary Organization: Two Neuropixels trajectory figures were merged into one supplementary figure, while a hardware-focused former main figure was moved to the supplement. The team also noted an ongoing discussion between Roberto and Sarah about aligning optotagging definitions in Supplementary Figure 5. 

Interactive Stimulus Figure: David said the static version of Supplementary Figure 6 is outdated and should be pared down to make fewer claims, while preserving the interactive component that lets users explore responses to different stimuli. Jerome asked David to revise it for later review. 

**SFN Poster Revision:** Jerome presented the SFN poster draft, and Alexander, Lucas, Marcel, Yuheng, and Farzaneh recommended simplifying it, removing dense quality-control content, clarifying panel navigation, and linking additional material through a QR code. 

Poster Content: The draft poster combines the experimental design, multimodal recording, quality-control figures, receptive-field analysis, standard-oddball results, data-release information, contribution credits, and an invitation to participate in the project. Jerome said the current version is too crowded and may need material removed. 

Simplification Proposal: Alexander recommended presenting a broad overview of the three recording techniques, four oddball types, and a prominent QR code directing visitors to the data. Lucas agreed that quality-control panels should be removed or minimized so the poster emphasizes what users can do with the dataset. 

Linked Materials: Marcel suggested placing figures or supporting content that cannot fit on the poster on an external website linked through the QR code. Jerome agreed that a QR code can be added even if the manuscript is not yet ready for a preprint archive. 

Poster Corrections: Yuheng identified inconsistent filter-gating criteria in the Ephys quality-control panel, particularly between panel 6D and other panels. Jerome agreed that the filtering should likely be harmonized. Farzaneh requested panel numbers to make the reading order clear, and Jerome agreed to add them. 

Publication Timing: Jerome said the figures are in relatively good shape but the manuscript text still needs substantial human revision, making publication before SFN uncertain. He plans to attend with an iPad so visitors can navigate the dynamic website. 

**Next Week Planning:** Jerome scheduled next week’s meeting around Nicholas’s receptive-field analysis and Alex and Sarah’s post-release analysis plan, with approximately 30 minutes allocated to each presentation. 

Scheduled Presentations: Nicholas will present his initial receptive-field drift and related analysis, while Alexander and Sarah will present their plan for follow-up analyses after the data-release manuscript. Jerome proposed allowing approximately 30 minutes for each presentation. 

Upcoming Figure Review: Karim and Nicholas also have an updated figure concerning receptor-field drift, and Jerome noted that meeting time should be reserved for both the follow-up analysis plan and that figure.
